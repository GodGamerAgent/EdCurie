For the upcoming Construct 2.0 Hackathon, our team is planning to build a product addressing a foundational challenge across the education system, including here at NST: the lack of truly personalized learning tracks. 

Every student comes with distinct strengths, gaps, learning paces, and educational backgrounds. Solving this solely through human mentoring is difficult to scale because tracking multi-dimensional learning variables per student is complex and human mentor bandwidth is limited.

We noticed that NST has already taken a proactive step toward this through the Neo Mentor feature. Rather than reinventing the wheel, we want to expand upon this foundation and propose high-impact architectural enhancements. 

The core features our implementation will introduce are:

1. ⁠ ⁠Knowledge Dependency Graph (DAG) for Root-Cause Remediation: Moving beyond linear course timetables by modeling concepts as an interconnected graph. When a student struggles with an advanced problem, the system automatically traces backward to identify and patch the exact prerequisite gap (e.g., flagging foundational vector algebra gaps when a student falters in rotational mechanics).

2. ⁠Dynamic Schedule Recalibration: An adaptive pacing engine that recalculates the student's daily syllabus path if they fall behind or accelerate, compressing low-yield areas and scheduling targeted catch-ups to keep the final milestone date locked.

3. On-Demand Weak-Point Targeted Mock Tests: An adaptive test generator that lets students generate full-length or modular mock tests instantly. The generator dynamically weights questions toward the student's historically high-error concepts and the highest-yield chapters of their target exam.

4. ⁠Behavioral & Learning-Style Adaptation: A system that models the student's cognitive patterns (e.g., visual intuition, mathematical proof, or step-by-step code) and automatically adjusts its explanations to match their preferred learning mode.

5. Zero-Prompt, Click-First UI (High Interaction Velocity): Eliminating the friction of long text prompts. Students interact via one-click context chips (e.g., "Show Intuition", "Give Hint 1", "Next Twin Problem", "Explain with Code") so the workflow remains seamless and focused.

6. Socratic Tutoring with Multi-Tiered Hint Escalation: Interactive problem assistance that avoids passive spoon-feeding by delivering progressive conceptual nudges before generating a twin question to verify genuine retention.

7. Spaced Retrieval & Error-Pattern Vault (FSRS-based): Continuous algorithmic tracking of mistakes and forgetting curves to schedule micro-recall sessions before critical concepts fade.

As Neo Mentor is closed source, we would like to request any high-level documentation or architectural overview regarding Neo Mentor's current feature set and technical design. Understanding its existing capabilities will help us align our implementation effectively and ensure our hackathon prototype serves as a complementary upgrade.
